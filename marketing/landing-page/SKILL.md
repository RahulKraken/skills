# Landing page

## Context

Rahul has rejected about 15 landing-page concepts across Meusic, Encognition and the portfolio, almost all for the same reason: they looked like "typical AI slop websites… like 2/10". The pattern Claude falls into is a feature page: a bold headline, a product screenshot, three feature cards, a purple glow. Rahul's references are the opposite: Apple, Nike, Stripe, Gentler Streak, OpenAI's Dots, Jony Ive's LoveFrom.

## The idea

- **Sell the change in the user's life, not the product.** "you never market the product, you always market what user's life is gonna look like". No feature lists, no screenshot hero. "I've never seen nike or apple talk about the features of their product."
- **Lead with the broad, magical promise.** "record anything you listen and we'll break it down for you", not a narrow feature.
- **Tell one story, like a film.** The page plays top to bottom like a scene, with a continuous camera: each section physically leads into the next ("going from one part of the screen to next doesn't do anything, it's just scrolling" was a rejection). The product can be the subject, or a character, but not a screen recording.
- **Protect the method.** Don't show the proprietary teaching or technical approach; keep shots high-level "so someone can't just copy".
- **The test is emotion.** Would a stranger trust it, be impressed, feel excited to try it? "if it's not evoking these emotions it's not good enough".

## The look

- **Modern minimalism with some fun.** "imagine how would apple make a website for garageband?" Clean, but never bland: "personality is gone" was also a rejection. Real artwork, tactile materials, specific subjects ("strat, i want a strat").
- **Micro-interactions where each element responds on its own** to the cursor and to clicks. Moving a whole screenshot is "lazy".
- **Soft edges**: fades and progressive blur instead of hard bands. Restraint in zoom and scale; nothing cropped.
- **Look outside the category**: Savee, landdding.com, Mobbin and Rahul's Notion inspiration list. "do not confine yourself to music related stuff".

## The ask

- **Hero CTA is a button only.** "writing email here is friction". The form comes after: email, plus at most two or three optional, skippable questions (for example, what song you'd learn first).
- **Brand name in the hero, not a slogan.** No nav, no scroll indicator, no "Early access · Invite only" badge.
- **Warm close**: "We're ironing out the rough edges. Join the waitlist and we'll let you know when it's ready for you."

## Work in two phases

1. **Design**: research references, build the page, then render it at desktop and phone width and look. For a scroll-driven page, capture several scroll positions, not just the first frame, and make the first frame strong on its own: it's what a stranger sees before deciding to scroll. If any art is drawn in code, look at it closely; Rahul has called code-drawn art "trash", so keep it simple and crafted, or use real artwork. Check the story reads with the text hidden, every section connects, nothing is cut off, and it doesn't look like a template.
2. **Text pass** with the `ui-copy` skill: list every visible line, keep only lines that carry the feeling or the ask, change words without touching the design.

Your message to Rahul: about 60 words, the feeling the page creates and how.

## Where this bends

- A docs or open-source project page (like Aftertrace) needs to explain how the product works, but through interactive figures, not essays.
- Never add biography, achievements or "launched/in progress" labels on Rahul's own pages ("it's all cringy").
